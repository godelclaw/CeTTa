Prime known issues
==================

Open defects in the Prime evaluator, with reproducers, observed behaviour
and current diagnosis.  An entry is removed by the change that fixes it,
together with a regression test.

Both issues below come from the fair first-answer scheduler (inline
observation quantum followed by fair native-control descent), commits
a9821431 and e882d604.  The opt merge that follows them does not touch
this code.


PK-1  Unbounded memory under fair native control
------------------------------------------------

Reproducers:

    ; A: a divergent alternative under a bounded prefix
    (= (spin) (spin))
    !(select 2 (superpose ((spin) (answer x))))

    ; B: a divergent alternative that grows its argument
    (= (cf $n) (cf (+ $n 1)))
    !(select 2 (superpose ((cf 0) (answer x))))

Both programs are expected to run forever: the bag has one member and the
prefix asks for two.  They should do so in bounded memory.

Observed (default build, 4 MiB collection budget):
    A: about 80 MB resident after 2 s, about 300 MB after 5 s.
    B: about 1.8 GB resident after 5 s.

Diagnosis:
  1. The native-control collector is called but always declines.  Its
     frame-kind admission (prime_native_control_collectable) omits
     PRIME_EVAL_STACK_FRAME_ALTERNATIVES, the frame that superpose and
     hyperpose push under native control, so no frontier containing a
     choice is ever collected.  It also refuses any frame with
     shared_need_wait, although a single-coordinator run evacuates and
     audits those frames completely.
  2. prime_native_control_schedule_publication passes the selected
     equation's full matcher environment to the scheduled body.  The
     stack and inline paths project it with
     bindings_project_control_continuation first, so dead equation-local
     bindings are carried only on the native path.
  3. In B the argument (+ $n 1) is never demanded by the pattern $n, so
     under call-by-need each step wraps one more suspended addition
     around the previous one.  That chain is live, so no collector can
     bound it.  Open question: evaluate a pure, total primitive
     application eagerly when all its operands are ready values (it cannot
     change any answer or effect), or accept growth linear in the number
     of steps for this program.


PK-2  once over deep single-path recursion is about twice as slow
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Reproducer:

    (= (lookup a) 1)
    (= (lookup b) 2)
    (= (lookup c) 3)
    (= (sum3) (+ (lookup a) (+ (lookup b) (lookup c))))
    (= (rep 0 $x) $x)
    (= (rep $n $x) (if (> $n 0) (rep (- $n 1) (+ $x (sum3))) $x))
    !(once (rep 3000 0))

Observed: about 0.40 s, against about 0.20 s with plain depth-first
descent.  The answer is correct.

Diagnosis:
  1. The descent is single-path down to (rep 0 ...), so the inline
     quantum is renewed all the way.  There both equations match.  While
     the first forces the accumulated argument (3000 nested suspended
     additions), the second waits, the quantum expires, and the inline
     attempt is discarded.  Native control then restarts the whole
     observation from (rep 3000 0) instead of adopting the live frontier.
  2. Under native control the two publications at (rep 0 ...) both demand
     the same shared argument cell.  The second starts its own force frame
     while the first is still in flight, so the 3000 additions are
     computed twice in lockstep.  The parallel force barrier that would
     share the in-flight force is active only for parallel controls.
  3. The prepared register loop
     (space_prepared_equation_run_register_loop and _recursion) is not
     bounded by the inline observation quantum, so the steps it takes are
     not charged to it.

Planned repair: adopt the inline frontier into native control on quantum
expiry; let a force frame in a single-coordinator control wait on an open
force of the same cell, world and fuel, and wake it with that force's
values; bound and charge the prepared register loop by the quantum.
