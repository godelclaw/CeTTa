Prime known issues
==================

Open defects in the Prime evaluator, with reproducers, observed behaviour
and current diagnosis.  An entry is removed by the change that fixes it,
together with a regression test.


PK-3  Library loops nest a C frame per item; a let in a loop body grows
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reproducers (n items written out as a literal list):

    ; A: add-atoms over a let-bound list of n rows (r 0) ... (r n-1)
    !(bind! &s (new-space))
    !(let $rows ((r 0) (r 1) ... (r 1499)) (add-atoms &s $rows))
    !(space-len &s)

    ; B: a pure filter
    !(size-atom (filter-atom (0 1 ... 999) $x (> $x 5)))

    ; C: a fold whose step binds with let
    !(foldl-atom (0 1 ... 1999) 0 $a $x (let $y (+ $x 1) (+ $a $y)))

Observed:
    A: (Incomplete stack-exhausted) from n = 1350, with 1346 rows stored;
       n = 1340 completes.
    B: (Incomplete stack-exhausted) at n = 1000; n = 180 completes.
    C: correct answers up to n = 2000, but the instruction count grows
       cubically: 6.46e9, 4.38e10, 1.39e11 and 3.20e11 at n = 500, 1000,
       1500 and 2000.  The same fold with an effectful step
       (let $u (add-atom &s (e $x)) ...), and map-atom with that step,
       complete at n = 1500 and exceed 120 s at n = 3000.

Diagnosis:
  1. The library loops are minimal-MeTTa programs,
     (chain (eval step) $v (eval (recur ...))), written for an eval that
     takes one step.  Prime's eval is the counit: it evaluates its argument
     to values with eval_for_caller, a nested native call, before running
     them.  The recursive (eval (recur ...)) therefore runs the rest of the
     loop inside that call, one C frame per item.
  2. At n = 500 about 80% of the time goes to cetta_frame_identity_retain
     and _release, bindings_frame_index_entry_free and
     bindings_frame_index_detach, and bindings_frame_schema_retain and
     _release.  Each step's let frame stays in the environment that the
     next step clones and releases, so that environment grows with the
     loop.

Repair direction: give the library spelling its own one-step eval (a
distinct head that the scope migrator writes into lib/prime), and drop a
let's frame when its scope ends, or prune bindings that neither the value
nor the continuation can reach.


PK-4  A streamed fold is quadratic in the number of answers
-----------------------------------------------------------

Reproducer:

    (= (range $lo $hi) (if (< $lo $hi) $lo (empty)))
    (= (range $lo $hi) (if (< (+ $lo 1) $hi) (range (+ $lo 1) $hi) (empty)))
    !(fold (range 0 4000) 0 $a $x (+ $a $x))

Observed: correct answers (499500, 1999000 and 7998000 for 1000, 2000 and
4000 answers) in 1.56e9, 4.73e9 and 1.62e10 instructions.  A
right-recursive choice generator,
(= (gen $n) (if (> $n 0) (superpose ($n (gen (- $n 1)))) (empty))), gives
(Incomplete stack-exhausted) for !(fold (gen 1000) 0 $a $x (+ $a 1)).

Diagnosis:
  1. The k-th answer is produced k levels down a chain of frames that
     forward answers.  Every native call made there classifies its target
     with prime_eval_stack_target_weak_head, which walks the owner chain:
     16.8% of the time at 2000 answers, 30% at 4000.
  2. Answers are deep-copied as they are forwarded
     (atom_deep_copy_session_new, about 12% of the time).

Repair direction: record a target's owner classification in its frame
when the frame is pushed, and let a frame that only forwards its child's
answers be replaced by its child.


PK-5  Held values reach sequence operations as a tag and their syntax
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Reproducers:

    (: hold (-> (Data Atom) (Data Atom)))
    (= (hold $x) $x)
    !(let $h (hold (c a b)) (superpose $h))
    !(let $h (hold (c c a)) (unique-atom $h))
    !(let $h (hold (c a b)) (sort-atom $h))
    !(let $h (hold ((+ 1 2) (+ 3 4))) (map-atom $h $x (w $x)))

Observed, in order:

    <internal-tag 13>, (c a b)
    (c c a)
    ((c a b) <internal-tag 13>)
    ((w <internal-tag 13>) (w (3 7)))

foldl-atom over (hold (c a b)) counts two elements.  car-atom, cdr-atom,
size-atom, index-atom and cons-atom read the payload, as the held-value
rule in atom.h says syntax observers do.

Diagnosis: a held value is CETTA_INTERNAL_TAG_PRIME_HELD applied to its
payload, and atom_sequence_view does not see through it.  Operations that
read elements through the view, or directly, see two elements, the tag and
the payload, and hand the payload out unheld, so held syntax runs without
an explicit evaluation.

Repair direction: the sequence view reads a held value's payload and
reports that it was held; each consumer wraps the fragments it hands out
as held, as car-atom and index-atom already do.  Open: whether a
rearranging operation (sort-atom, unique-atom) returns a held expression
or an unheld expression of held fragments.
